01

The problem

Students already resell to each other — badly. The tools they use weren't built for a campus.

  • Strangers & scams

    Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist mean randoms from across the city, sketchy meetups, and "is this still available?" into the void.

  • Move-out is a landfill

    Dorms empty in May and nearly every Uptown lease ends July 31 — so the neighborhood clears out in a single week, and good furniture, mini-fridges, and textbooks hit the dumpster because there's nowhere fast to pass them on.

  • Students overpay

    The same textbook resells semester after semester, but retail buyback pennies-on-the-dollar keeps value leaking off campus.

03

The solution

One place, gated to UC, for the whole lifecycle of student stuff.

What it is

A campus-native marketplace

Buy, sell, donate, and recover lost items — textbooks, dorm gear, bikes, tickets, and lost-and-found — in one app built for the two-minute walk between classes.

Why it's safe

Trust designed in, not bolted on

  • Accounts gated to a verified @uc.edu email
  • Meetups at named, public campus spots
  • Ratings, reporting, and moderation from day one
04

Why now

UC already funds an answer to this problem. It runs entirely downstream.

Today

Haul it to a dumpster on McMillan

Right now — July 24 – August 2, 2026 — the university and eight partners are running Uptown Waste Diversion at 121 E McMillan: extra dumpsters, e-waste bins, a donation drop-off. It works. But it is ten days a year in one location, and everything it handles has already become garbage.

With 2nd Time Around

A sophomore two blocks away takes it

All year, every dorm and every Uptown block — and it never becomes waste at all, because someone who needed it got there first. We are the upstream half of a program UC already pays for, which makes this an extension of existing institutional work rather than a new line item.

The campus is also the moat: everyone is verified so nobody is a stranger, everyone is a five-minute walk away so liquidity is instant, and the academic calendar guarantees demand spikes twice a year. Win one campus, then replicate.

06

Business model

Free for students, forever. We never take a cut of a student-to-student trade — that trust is the product. Revenue comes from the edges.

Today

$0 to students, no transaction fees

A $40 textbook puts $40 in a student's pocket. Adoption and trust first; monetization never touches the core exchange.

Revenue paths

Monetize the edges, not the trade

  • Featured / bumped listings during peak weeks
  • Verified local partners — housing, textbook buyback, bike shops
  • Sponsored move-out donation drives
  • University sustainability-office partnership & reporting
